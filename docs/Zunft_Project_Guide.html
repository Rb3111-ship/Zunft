1. The idea

Zunft (German for "guild", the traditional association of craftsmen) is a phone app for small German craft businesses - Handwerksbetriebe. The first customer we are designing for is an electrician with 1 to 10 employees in Hamburg.

The problem it solves

Picture Thorsten, an electrician. He spends the day on building sites: installing sockets, fixing fuse boxes. When he gets home at 7 pm he still has to write quotes and invoices - typing numbers into a computer after a long day. Many craftsmen hate this and lose hours (and money) every week. Some forget to invoice at all.

The solution

1. Thorsten opens the job (say "Müller, Keller") and speaks into his phone"New socket in the basement, two hours, material about 40 euros." The customer is already fixed by the job.
▼
2. The app turns speech into text, then an AI turns text into a structured draftWork items, hours, material, prices - all separated correctly. Several notes (even over several days) collect on the same job.
▼
3. When the job is done, Thorsten checks all its notes as one draft, fixes anything, taps "PDF"A professional German invoice, ready to send by WhatsApp or e-mail.

Total effort: about one minute instead of twenty.

Why German craft businesses specifically?

  • German invoice rules are strict. An invoice needs specific information (address, tax number, invoice number, and so on). The app knows these rules so the craftsman does not have to.
  • A tax rule called § 35a EStG. Private customers can deduct part of a craftsman's bill from their income tax - but only the labour part, not the material. So the invoice must show labour and material separately. Done wrong, the customer loses money and complains. The app separates them automatically.
  • Craftsmen work where there is bad network (basements, new buildings). So the app must work without internet and catch up later.

The long-term vision (three layers)

1. The hookVoice note to quote/invoice PDF (what we are building now, "Phase 1").In progress
2. Overflow networkWhen a business is fully booked it can pass jobs to nearby colleagues, with a referral fee ("Phase 3").Later

2. The big picture

The whole system has three pieces. Understanding these three is 80% of understanding the project.

PIECE 1 - The phone app (Flutter)What Thorsten holds in his hand. Screens, buttons, the microphone, a small private database on the phone. Lives in the folder zunft_app/.
▲ ▼   talks over the internet   ▲ ▼
PIECE 2 - The cloud (Supabase)A rented online "back room": the main database, the login system, and file storage for recordings and PDFs. Lives in the folder supabase/.
▲ ▼   asks for help   ▲ ▼
PIECE 3a - DeepgramSpecialist company that turns speech into written text (German).
PIECE 3b - Google GeminiAI that reads that text and understands it: who is the customer, what work, what price.
Analogy: a restaurant The phone app is the dining room - what the guest sees and touches. Supabase is the kitchen and pantry - it stores everything and does the work guests should not see. The "Edge Function" (explained later) is the head chef who takes an order and calls two specialists: Deepgram is the listener who writes down exactly what was said, Gemini is the clerk who understands the note and fills out the form correctly.

Why not put everything on the phone?

  • Secret keys. Deepgram and Gemini charge money per use. Their passwords (called API keys) must never be stored in the app, because anyone can pull them out of an app. They live only in the cloud piece.
  • Shared data. If the business has several employees, they all need to see the same customers and jobs. That needs one central copy (the cloud).
  • Backup. Phones get lost and broken. The cloud copy survives.

Why not put everything in the cloud?

Because job sites have bad reception. So the phone keeps its own small copy of the data and works with it. When the internet is there, the two copies are synchronised ("sync"). This is called an offline-first app, and it shapes a lot of the work in Step 4.

The journey of one voice note

1
Thorsten chooses the job and records. The phone saves an audio file (together with the job) and uploads it to Supabase storage in a folder named after his business.
2
The app calls the Edge Function ("please process this recording and store it on this job") and shows its login proof.
3
The Edge Function checks who is asking, finds his business, and downloads the audio.
4
Deepgram turns the audio into German text.
5
Gemini reads the text and the business's usual hourly rate (no customer data at all) and returns structured positions.
6
The Edge Function double-checks the AI's answer (AI can make mistakes), calculates the tax split and totals, and saves the result as a note on the job (with who recorded it and when).
7
The phone shows the note on the job. When the work is done, all notes of the job become one draft; Thorsten corrects anything, then issues the invoice and the PDF.
Where we areEvery stage of this journey now exists in code, including the last one: a checked draft becomes a numbered, locked invoice with a proper PDF. What has not happened yet is one real end-to-end run with real keys on a real phone - see Chapter 11.

6. What was built, step by step

The plan had seven steps. Six are complete. Each section below says what was done, why, and how it works.

Step 1 - Foundation done

Goal: make the project a proper, buildable, documented project.

  • Git created. The folder is now a Git project, with a .gitignore list that keeps junk (build output) and secret keys out of the history. Nothing has been "committed" (saved) yet - I only commit when you ask.
  • Phone folders added (android/, ios/, windows/) by running Flutter's own scaffolding command.
  • A real bug fixed: the colour theme used a name that the current Flutter no longer accepts, so the app did not compile. Fixed.
  • README and .env.example written: how to start the project and which secret settings are needed (with the safe rule: secrets only ever in the cloud, never in the app).
  • Internet permission added for Android. Android's default template only allows internet access in test builds. A real release build would have been unable to reach the cloud at all.

Step 2 - Backend hardening done

Goal: make the cloud side safe and correct. Two parts: the Edge Function, and the database.

2a. The Edge Function process-capture

Now there is a doorman: he checks the wristband (login token), looks up which business you belong to himself, and only lets you into your own pantry.
ImprovementWhat it means

Acts as the userIt no longer uses the all-powerful "master key". Every database action runs with the user's own limited rights, so the database locks (RLS) protect each business.
Folder rule for recordingsA recording must live under <business id>/.... Nobody can ask it to transcribe another business's audio.
Size limitsAudio max 10 MB, text max 4,000 characters - protects against runaway bills.
Switched to GeminiAsks Gemini for an answer in a strict JSON shape. The model name is a setting.
Never trust the AI blindlyA checker (_shared/draft.ts) rejects nonsense: unknown item types, negative quantities, absurd prices, or a customer ID the AI invented. If the answer is bad it retries once, then returns a friendly error.
Prompt-injection defenceThe spoken text could contain "ignore your rules and..." (someone could say it on purpose). The text is wrapped in labelled tags and the AI is told it is only data, never instructions.
No leakingErrors shown to the app are short and generic; technical details go to the server log only.
Bugs fixedEmpty speech result no longer crashes it; audio type is detected from the file; failing to save a job now stops with an error instead of silently continuing.

2b. The database migration

A migration is a saved set of database changes. We kept the original and added a second one, 20261002000000_harden_schema.sql.

ImprovementWhat it means
Safe invoice numbersNumbers look like RE-2026-0001, RE-2026-0002... The old method ("count existing + 1") could give two invoices the same number if two people saved at once. The new method keeps a counter per business, type and year that is locked while it is being increased - like a single ticket machine with one person at a time. If a save fails, the number is handed back, so there are no gaps (German tax law dislikes gaps).
Better locks (RLS)Old: one broad rule per table. New: separate rules for reading, adding, changing and deleting. A job cannot point to another business's customer. Only an owner can change roles; nobody can promote themselves. The audit log can be added to but never edited or deleted.
Tamper-proof invoices (GoBD)Once an invoice is "locked" it can no longer be edited or deleted, and the lock time is stamped automatically. The invoice number can never be changed.
Onboarding functionA new function create_business_for_user() creates the business and its owner in one go (both or neither). Apps cannot insert into those tables directly.
Invoices for brand-new customersA document can now store a copy of the recipient (name and address) instead of needing a customer record. That also freezes what was printed even if the customer is edited later.
Sanity checksAllowed statuses/roles are enforced; totals must add up (net + VAT = gross); "last changed" times update automatically.
Safe helper functionsThe internal helpers were hardened and cannot be called by logged-out visitors.
File storage rulesRecordings and PDFs are private; each business can only touch files in its own folder.

Step 4 - Local database and sync done

Goal: real customers and jobs, working without internet.

Analogy: a notebook and a filing clerk Thorsten carries a notebook (the phone's local database). He writes everything in it immediately, wherever he is. Every new note also goes into a "to-send" tray (the outbox). Whenever there is internet, a filing clerk (the sync engine) carries the tray's contents to the head office, and brings back any new papers from there. A note leaves the tray only after the office confirms it arrived.

What you see

  • Aufträge (Jobs) tab: list of jobs with customer and coloured status; a summary of drafts / in progress / completed; add, edit, delete.
  • Kunden (Customers) tab: list with search (name, street, city, phone - several words allowed), add, edit, delete (asks first).
  • Sync tile in the Betrieb tab: honest status - "Synchronisiert", "Offline", "Please sign in again", or "Some changes were rejected", with the last sync time and how many changes are waiting. Tap it to sync now.

How the sync works, in a worked example

1
In a basement with no signal, Thorsten adds the customer "Schmidt". It appears in the list immediately and a note "upload customer Schmidt" lands in the outbox.
2
He then adds a job for Schmidt. A second note lands in the outbox.
3
Back in his van the phone gets signal. The sync engine wakes up. Order matters: customers are sent before jobs (a job pointing at a customer the cloud has not seen would be refused). Deletes go the other way round: jobs first, then customers.
4
Each note leaves the outbox only when the cloud confirms it. If the cloud refuses one (e.g. a rule is violated) that note stays, with the reason, and the others continue. If the connection drops, nothing is lost - it retries later.
5
Then the engine pulls: it asks the cloud "what changed since I last asked?" (a bookmark, called a watermark, remembers where it stopped), and also removes local rows that were deleted elsewhere.

When does a sync happen?

When the app opens, a couple of seconds after any change, as soon as the network returns, every 5 minutes, and whenever you tap the sync tile. Only one sync runs at a time.

What if two people edit the same thing?

The rule is "last write wins": whoever syncs last decides. A change still waiting in the outbox is never overwritten by incoming data. This is simple and fine for a small team, but two people editing the same customer at the same moment could overwrite each other.

Two bugs found and fixed during the build

Lost editIf you edited a record while the previous version was being uploaded, the app could have deleted your newer edit from the outbox when the older upload finished. Each outbox entry now carries a revision number, and it is only removed if that number is unchanged. A test proves it - and I confirmed the test fails when the protection is turned off.
Accidental deletion at scaleThe cloud returns at most 1,000 rows at a time. The "remove what was deleted elsewhere" step would have mistaken customer number 1,001+ for deleted ones. The list is now fetched page by page.

Shared phone safety

The phone remembers which business its cached data belongs to. If a different business logs in on the same phone, the old cache is wiped first so nobody sees someone else's customers.

Tooling note

Drift needs a "code generator" that writes helper code from the table definitions. It crashed at first because its internals were older than your Flutter version. I upgraded it and removed a second generator (riverpod_generator) that nothing used. The generated file is saved in the project so you do not need to run it again unless the tables change.

Technical details, in plain words

  • Recording format: speech is recorded mono, 16 kHz, AAC (a small compressed format): about half a megabyte per minute. Noise suppression and automatic volume are switched on for building sites.
  • Where the audio goes: into private cloud storage, in a folder named after the business (<business id>/<note id>.m4a), matching the cloud function's folder rule.
  • Phone permissions: Android needs RECORD_AUDIO declared; iPhone needs a sentence explaining why the microphone is used (otherwise the app would crash when asking). Both were added.
  • Database change on the phone: a new table for queued notes. The local database moved from version 1 to 2 with a proper upgrade step, so existing installs keep their data.
  • Cloud change: a third migration adds a "capture id" column with a uniqueness rule, and the Edge Function now recognises repeats (and handles two identical requests arriving at the same moment by keeping one result).

Bugs the tests caught while building this

The microphone could have stayed onIf the sheet was dismissed while recording, the cleanup code used a Riverpod feature that is not allowed at that moment. It would have thrown an error and left the microphone recording. The test "closing the sheet while recording" found it; the code now takes what it needs at start-up.
Duplicate item IDsAI drafts have no item IDs, so the app invented them from the clock, in milliseconds. Several items created in the same millisecond got the same ID, which would have broken editing and deleting. Each item now gets a unique ID.
File access could not be testedReading the audio file straight from disk meant the processing never finished inside a test (real disk access cannot run inside the test simulator's fake clock). File access now sits behind a small interface, like the microphone and the cloud, so each can be swapped for a pretend version in tests.

3

4

5

Why you need internet to finalize

Analogy: the ticket machine at head officeDraft documents can be edited anywhere, even in a basement. But invoice numbers must be consecutive with no gaps (German tax auditors check this). Only one place can guarantee that: a single ticket machine at head office (the cloud). So the final step needs a connection. If it fails, the app says so, keeps all your edits, and you simply try again later.

How the numbers are truly gapless

In Step 2 the number was handed out by one cloud call and the document was saved by a second call. If the connection died in between, a number would be used up with no document behind it: a gap. Now one cloud function (create_document) does everything in a single transaction: check the input, calculate the totals, take the next number, save the document. Either all of it happens or none of it does. Retrying the same request (same document ID) returns the same document and the same number, never a second one.

Photographing receipts (Belege)

A PDF is read the same way as a photo.

  • Reading: the cloud function sends the photo to Gemini, which only copies what is printed (items, quantities, prices, the VAT rate, the total).   • Markup (Aufschlag): under Betrieb → Bearbeiten → Standardwerte you can enter a percentage, for example 10. Material that comes from a receipt then goes into the invoice at the purchase price plus that percentage; the review screen says so, and every price can still be changed there. Empty = no markup. Material you speak yourself is never changed.

Mistakes found and fixed while building this

A hole in my own validationIn database language, "missing" is not the same as "wrong": a check like type NOT IN (...) silently passes when the type is missing altogether. A request with no positions at all could have produced an empty invoice. Every field is now checked for "missing" explicitly, and the tests try each missing field one by one and confirm that nothing is stored and no number is used up.
A mistake from Step 2I had frozen locked documents so completely that nobody could mark an invoice "sent" or "paid". The rule now freezes the content but still allows the status workflow (migration 4 fixes this).
Due dates off by a dayComputing "today + 30 days" as 30 × 24 hours is wrong when the clocks change (last Sunday of October): the due date lands one day early. Now it counts calendar days. A test crossing the clock change caught it.
A spinner behind the error messageWhen finalizing failed, the progress spinner kept turning behind the error box until you closed it. The busy state is now reset before any message is shown.
A newly issued document could vanish for a momentSync removes local documents that the server no longer lists. If you issued a document while a sync was running, that list was out of date and the new document was removed locally (it came back at the next sync). Sync now never removes a document that is newer than what it has already seen.
A quirk of the database languageA CASE ... THEN inside an IF ... THEN condition is cut off at the first THEN. Found by the tests; the value is now computed first.

Step 6b - The redesign: one job, many notes done

Why: you asked good questions after Step 6, and they exposed a weakness. In the first version every voice note became its own draft, and the AI guessed the customer from the spoken name. With many people called Müller that is dangerous, and a job spread over several days would have produced a pile of small drafts. So the working of the app was changed, before any real customer used it.

The new idea in one picture

▼

▼

Technical details, in plain words

  • Fifth migration: notes get an author, a "used in document" link and a "removed" flag. A note cannot be attributed to a colleague or created as already used. Removing a note is a small guarded cloud function (only inside your own business, only if not yet billed).
  • The cloud function now requires a job and no longer creates jobs. It reads no customer data and its prompt contains no customer list. It returns only a name hint.
  • The invoice-making function (create_document) accepts the note IDs and uses them up atomically.
  • Privacy effect: the AI no longer receives customer names, first names or cities from your database. It only gets what is spoken.
  • Tests: 9 new database tests (two workers, no double billing, foreign notes untouched), the cloud-function tests reworked (now 11), and a rewritten app test set (213, including the duplicate check): picking a job, banner, "Ändern", offline notes for several jobs, job created offline, combining notes from two workers into one invoice, a job without customer, notes used up.

Duplicate customers: a name alone is not enough

Many people are called Müller, so the app does not treat a matching name as "same customer". When you create a customer by hand it compares more than the name:

SituationWhat the app does
Same name and same address (street, number, postcode), or same phone, or same e-mailClear warning "Diesen Kunden gibt es schon". You can cancel or create it anyway.
Same name, but a different addressNo warning at all: it is a different person.
Same name, but the address (or phone/e-mail) is missing on one sideA softer question "Gibt es diesen Kunden schon?". You decide.
Different name, even at the same addressNo warning (a family or a firm at one address).

Spelling differences are ignored: "Müller" = "Mueller", "Müller, Thomas" = "Thomas Müller", "Hauptstraße 5" = "Hauptstr. 5", phone numbers in any format. House numbers do matter (no. 5 is not no. 7).

When an invoice is issued for a customer typed in on the review screen, the app only reuses an existing customer in the clear case (same name and same address/phone/e-mail). In the unclear case it keeps them separate, because a wrong automatic merge would put an invoice on the wrong person's record, which is worse than a duplicate.

Is Zunft for one tradesman or for small companies?

Both, but today only the "boss" side exists. The database was designed for a business with several people (every person has a profile that points to a business, with a role). But the app currently only has screens for the owner. There is no screen to add employees, and no time tracking.

My recommendationDo not build a time-tracking system or a desktop program yet. Time tracking is a whole separate product area (existing tools such as Clockodo or Toggl already do it well). First prove the core idea: voice note becomes invoice. Later, the cheap way to give the boss an overview is a web page version of this same app (Flutter can do that) plus a "download as spreadsheet" button that existing time tools can read. One app, not two.

Are we allowed to send customer data to outside companies?

I am not a lawyer, so treat this as a briefing and not as legal advice. As I understand the GDPR (DSGVO):

  • It is allowed to use outside companies to process data, if you have a signed data-processing agreement (Auftragsverarbeitungsvertrag, AVV) with each one, your customers can read a privacy policy, and any transfer outside the EU has a legal safeguard.
  • Today we use two such companies: Deepgram (voice to text) and Google (Gemini). Both are US companies. Both offer the paperwork, but you must actually accept it in their dashboards.
  • Free versus paid matters. As far as I know, Google's free Gemini tier may use your data to improve its products. For real customers, use the paid tier (billing turned on), where Google says it does not train on your data. Please confirm this in Google's current terms.

Worker goes from job to job with the internet off

This is what the queue was built for. Every recording gets its own unique number and is saved on the phone. When the internet returns, the app uploads them one by one, in order. A failed upload retries three times, and a real rejection is shown clearly and nothing silently disappears. Customers and jobs typed offline sync the same way.

Honest limits: (1) issuing the final numbered invoice needs internet; (2) a recording is only queued when you tap stop, so a phone crash during a recording loses it; (3) I have tested all this with simulated networks, not with a real phone in a real basement. "Each worker has their own customers, so two people can't collide"

Small correction: customers belong to the business, not to a worker. Two employees see the same customer list. Two good things: recordings can never collide (each phone has its own queue and every note is stored on the job it was recorded for). Two weak spots: two people may create the same customer twice (the app now warns when name and address, phone or e-mail match, but not for same-name people at different addresses), or edit the same customer at the same time (the last save wins). A "merge duplicates" function is a sensible later feature.

Is the voice recording kept, for checking the AI?

Partly. The text the AI heard (the transcript) is saved with every job, and the audio file stays in the cloud storage. Only the copy on the phone is deleted once the cloud has it. Done: every note on the job screen has "Was wurde gesagt?", which shows the written text, so the craftsman can check what the AI understood. Missing: a "play recording" button. I have put it on the list. (Under the GDPR you should also decide how long audio is kept; I suggest deleting it after a set time, for example 90 days.)

Do 10 notes for Herr Müller all land on one invoice?

They do now - on purpose, and only on the right job. This was your best observation, and it has been built (see Step 6b). In the first version every note was its own draft and the AI guessed the customer. Now:

  • You choose the job first; the job knows its customer. A big banner in the recording sheet always shows "AUFNAHME FÜR: customer / job", with "Ändern" if you picked wrong.
  • The AI never picks a customer, so "Frau Müller" can no longer turn out to be the wrong Müller.

  • A note can be removed from the job (bin icon) if it is wrong.

And to your earlier thought "each worker has their own copy": invoices belong to the company (one number sequence, one invoice per job). What each worker keeps is their name on every note, so you can see who was there and when.
